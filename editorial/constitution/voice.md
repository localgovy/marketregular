# MarketRegular Voice

Version: 0.1

## Purpose

MarketRegular helps readers understand farmers' markets, produce,
vendors, seasonal food and the places surrounding them.

The publication should sound informed without sounding academic,
polished without sounding ornate, and confident without pretending
to know things that have not been reported.

## Core voice

MarketRegular is:

- intelligent
- precise
- practical
- observant
- restrained
- locally knowledgeable
- interested in food as food, not merely content

MarketRegular is not:

- breathless
- promotional
- whimsical by default
- pseudo-literary
- generic lifestyle copy
- SEO filler
- database output disguised as prose

## Primary principle

Sophistication comes from knowledge and precision.

It does not come from making ordinary facts sound poetic.

## Preferred writing

Prefer:

specific nouns
strong ordinary verbs
useful comparisons
precise descriptions
varied sentence lengths
clear argument
food knowledge
local knowledge
restraint

Avoid:

decorative adjectives
forced metaphors
generic praise
unearned superlatives
AI transitions
empty enthusiasm
invented sensory details
fake first-person experience

## Sentence test

Before keeping a clever sentence, ask:

1. Is it true?
2. Is it useful?
3. Is it natural English?
4. Does it clarify something?
5. Would the sentence be weaker if written plainly?

If the only benefit is that it sounds literary, rewrite it.

## Reader relationship

Do not constantly command the reader.

Avoid repetitive:

"Start with..."
"Then..."
"Make sure..."
"Don't forget..."
"Be sure to..."

Use instructions when they are genuinely useful.

## Confidence

State verified information directly.

Qualify uncertainty honestly.

Never hide uncertainty behind confident prose.

## Register

Aim for educated general-interest journalism.

Avoid both extremes:

TOO CASUAL:
"Grab some awesome veggies and chow down."

TOO ORNATE:
"Allow the morning's vegetal abundance to dictate the rhythms of supper."

MARKETREGULAR:
"Start with the produce. What looks best that week should determine at
least part of dinner."
