# Best farmers’ markets for vegans in Toronto

A plant-based shopper does not need a market that hangs a single vegan banner over the gate. What you need is a hall where the produce is worth a full lap, the kitchen stalls cook to the Ontario season, and you can fill a week of meals without treating every table like a trivia quiz. Toronto has that pattern now — Saturday at Brick Works and The Stop, a Riverdale morning at Withrow, a deep Sunday at Leslieville, a rare Monday at Sorauren — if you shop the way the best food writing has always told people to shop produce: walk first, ask what is peaking, buy what you will actually cook, then add the prepared food that makes the bag feel complete.

Hours and stalls below come from [MarketRegular](https://www.marketregular.com). Tap a name for the live listing. Lineups rotate week to week, so treat this as published roster evidence, not a promise that every stall will be out on your visit. When a dish might hide butter, yogurt, cheese, or honey, ask. A vendor tagged vegan on our site is published with that focus; “menu vegan” means some items qualify and the rest of the board may not.

## How to shop the hall

The most refined market writing — Bon Appétit’s test-kitchen habits, GrowNYC’s greenmarket guides, the seasonal essays that treat a Saturday morning as craft — keeps returning to the same moves, and they matter more when your cart is almost all plants.

Do a full lap before you buy anything. The first handsome bunch of kale is not always the best bunch that morning. At Brick Works or Leslieville, that walk is the difference between a good haul and a great one: you compare colour and snap, taste when a sample is offered, and only then commit. Ask growers what is *excellent* this week, not merely what is in season. Tomatoes may still be on the table while the grower is already steering you toward squash and roots; that conversation is how plant-based cooks find the odd varieties the supermarket never bothers to stock.

Buy for the next few days, not for a fantasy week. Peak greens and soft fruit die in the crisper when you overbuy. Go in with a loose plan — greens, fruit, something prepared for lunch — and leave room for one thing you have never cooked. Cash still helps at many farm stalls; a real bag or backpack keeps your hands free. If you will not use beet tops or carrot greens, ask to have them cut (or take them home for pesto and a hot pan). Prepared food is the bridge that makes Toronto’s vegan market scene work: dips, patties, bakery, and plant-based plates so you eat while you shop and leave with dinner already started.

## Saturday: Brick Works

[Evergreen Brick Works Saturday Farmers Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/evergreen-brick-works-saturday-farmers-market) · 550 Bayview Avenue · **8 AM–1 PM** · year-round

Outdoors under the pavilions through the warm months, indoors in the TD Future Cities Centre from November into spring. It is Toronto’s largest year-round Saturday farmers’ market, and for plant-based shoppers it is also the densest published vegan stack in our catalog — not because every table is vegan, but because you can do a serious produce lap and still fill the rest of the bag from kitchen stalls that cook plants on purpose.

Start with the farms. Evergreen’s vendors sit within a tight local radius, which is the whole point of shopping here instead of a chain produce aisle: you can ask what came off the field this week, what is fading, and what they would cook tonight. Alma, Earth and City, and Natural Japaneats are published vegan. Molly B’s is the dedicated gluten-free bakery (vegan goods on the board — ask which). Bashy Patties, Happy Pops, Neighbours Feeding Neighbours, and Woodfolk Cider often round out a plant-based Saturday; confirm the dish that week. Bashy is a patty stall with a meat tag, so do not assume the board.

**Vendors to look for:**

- [Natural Japaneats](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/natural-japaneats) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Molly B’s Gluten-Free Kitchen](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/molly-b-s-gluten-free-kitchen) · 8 AM–1 PM

If you only have one big market morning a month, this is the hall where a full winter lap still feels worth the trip into the Don Valley.

## Saturday: The Stop

[The Stop’s Farmers’ Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/the-stops-farmers-market) · 601 Christie Street · **8 AM–1 PM** · year-round

The same Saturday window all year, inside and around the old streetcar barns. The Stop is a community food organization, and you feel that in how the market is built: local farms, prepared food with real cultural range, and a Saturday that does not vanish when the temperature drops. For vegans west of downtown, it is one of the cleanest year-round bets in the city.

Do the produce tables while they still look like morning, then eat your way through the kitchen stalls. Earth and City, Chic Peas Veg, and Paratha Begum are published vegan. Atiba Farm bridges farm and kitchen and also shows up Sundays at the Afro-Caribbean market in Fairbank. Beytoot’s Lebanese hummus, baba ghanouj, muhammara, and toom are written as vegan recipes, but the stall is not published under a vegan tag — confirm that week. Mad Mexican is the same ask-the-board case. Arth TO is tagged vegan and gluten-free, and also sells Tuesdays at Trinity Bellwoods.

**Vendors to look for:**

- [Earth and City](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/earth-city) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Chic Peas Veg](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/chic-peas-veg) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Paratha Begum](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/paratha-begum) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Atiba Farm](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/atiba-farm) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Beytoot](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/beytoot) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Mad Mexican](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/mad-mexican) · 8 AM–1 PM
- [Arth TO](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/arth-to) · 8 AM–1 PM

The barns make this one of the cozier cold-weather Saturdays in Toronto. Arrive near open if greens are the reason you came.

## Saturday: Withrow Park

[Withrow Park Farmers’ Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/withrow-park-farmers-market) · 725 Logan Avenue · **9 AM–1 PM** · May 30–Oct 17

East-enders treat it as weekly grocery because the farm tables are deep; plant-based shoppers stay because the second half of the bag can be genuinely fun. Lap the produce first — compare stalls the way you would for peaches in August — then finish at Live On Chocolate (vegan, organic, gluten-free desserts and confections), Alma, and Crazy D’s. Beytoot is here too, with the same ask-that-week caution as at The Stop. Happy Pops, Mad Mexican, The Fritter Shop, and Nepali Momo are on the roster; Nepali Momo is tagged vegan and also meat, so ask which dumplings are plant-based that day.

**Vendors to look for:**

- [Live On Chocolate](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/live-on-chocolate) · 9 AM–1 PM

## Sunday: Leslieville

[The Leslieville Farmers’ Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/the-leslieville-farmers-market) · 150 Greenwood Avenue · **9 AM–2 PM** · May 10–Oct 25

The outdoor season in Greenwood Park. Once you count bakery, prepared food, and dips together, it is one of the deepest plant-based Sundays in the city. It is also easy to confuse with the indoor East End Food Hub season on Gerrard — same organizers, different address and calendar. This section is the park.

Walk the farm tables first (Neighbours Feeding Neighbours when listed), then build the kitchen half of the bag. Alma, Earth and City, Bad Attitude Bread, Portobello Road, From Lex’s Pantry, and Gebeta are published vegan. From Lex’s is also tagged gluten-free. Beytoot again: dips to confirm that week. House of Empanadas often has plant-based fillings — confirm which ones that Sunday. When the outdoor season ends, check the [East End Food Hub listing](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/leslieville-farmers-market-east-end-food-hub) separately rather than showing up at Greenwood expecting farm stalls.

## Monday: Sorauren

[Sorauren Farmers’ Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/sorauren-farmers-market) · 50 Wabash Avenue · **3 PM–7 PM**

Most Toronto farmers’ markets take Monday off. This one does not. It runs in the park near Roncesvalles, which makes it the plant-based shopper’s salvage day when the weekend got away from you. The timing is built for an after-work bag: greens, something prepared, home before dark in summer.

Vital Life Vegan is the published plant-based kitchen focus here. Earth and City is on the Monday roster as well.

**Vendors to look for:**

- [Vital Life Vegan](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/vital-life-vegan) · 3 PM–7 PM
- [Earth and City](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/earth-city) · 3 PM–7 PM

In the cold months the same Monday slot often moves indoors to [Sorauren at Henderson Brewery](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/sorauren-farmers-market-henderson-brewery) on Sterling Road. Confirm which door is live before you go — same day and hours pattern, different address.

## Thursday: Dufferin Grove

[Dufferin Grove Organic Farmers’ Market](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/dufferin-grove-organic-farmers-market) · 875 Dufferin Street · **3 PM–7 PM** · year-round

Year-round at the clubhouse. If midweek is when you actually cook, this is the organic hall that keeps going when outdoor markets pack up. Earth and City anchors the plant-based prepared side; the rest of the trip should be produce. Ask what is peaking, buy greens you will eat within a few days, and treat Thursday here as grocery rather than an outing.

**Vendors to look for:**

- [Earth and City](https://www.marketregular.com/vendors/earth-city) · 3 PM–7 PM

## A plant-based week that actually gets cooked

Open one Saturday at Brick Works or The Stop at 8 AM: lap produce while it still looks like morning, then fill prepared food and bakery. If you live east, shift that same pattern to Withrow at 9. On Sunday, use Leslieville for bakery and Earth and City depth, or run Eglinton Way early and Fairbank later for Atiba and Caribbean stalls. When the weekend fails you, Sorauren on Monday (or Henderson indoors in winter) is the salvage market with Vital Life Vegan. Thursday is Dufferin Grove for year-round organic grocery, or Underpass when you want more kitchen density in season. Sharing a kitchen with mixed diets? Brick Works Saturday still wins — Molly B’s beside Earth and City and Alma.

The refined way to shop any of these halls is still the produce editor’s way. Tags tell you which door to walk toward; the conversation at the table tells you what goes in the bag. For gluten-free and vegan overlaps, start with Molly B’s and Live On Chocolate, where those labels are actually published.

- **Saturday, 8 AM:** [Brick Works](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/evergreen-brick-works-saturday-farmers-market) or [The Stop](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/the-stops-farmers-market). East of downtown, [Withrow](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/withrow-park-farmers-market) at **9 AM**.
- **Sunday:** [Leslieville](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/the-leslieville-farmers-market) at Greenwood, or [Eglinton Way](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/eglinton-way-bia-farmers-market) early and [Fairbank](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/afro-caribbean-farmers-market) later.
- **Monday:** [Sorauren](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/sorauren-farmers-market), or [Henderson](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/sorauren-farmers-market-henderson-brewery) indoors in winter.
- **Thursday:** [Dufferin Grove](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/dufferin-grove-organic-farmers-market) year-round, or [Underpass Park](https://www.marketregular.com/markets/underpass-park-farmers-market) in season.
- **MarketRegular Tip:** Do not empty two full farm markets before noon. Withrow’s produce on Saturday and Leslieville’s bakery on Sunday is the east-end split that actually gets cooked.
